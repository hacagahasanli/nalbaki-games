Case studies, with the numbers.

Three of our own games, measured the way a player feels them: how fast they open, how little they download, how smoothly they run and how fair they stay online. Load times come from a scripted browser on the shipped builds, sizes from the build itself. No estimates, no invented metrics.

Portal buildthree.jsCrazyGames SDK v3

Parking Lot: a portal-ready build

A careful-driving race against three bots, rebuilt as one static folder for CrazyGames: straight into the race, ads only between rounds, and graphics that scale down on weak devices.

347 KBthe whole game over the wire, gzipped
2.5 sto the race screen on fast 4G
6.6 son slow 4G with a 4× slower CPU
60 fpsheld on a laptop at 1280×720, the display’s limit
< 3.3 msof CPU per frame, even with the whole car park in view
3quality tiers, picked to suit the device

What was built

  • A small platform interface with a CrazyGames SDK v3 adapter (loading, gameplay events, midgame and rewarded ads, mute, device type, saved data) and a no-op fallback when the SDK is missing or takes over 4 s. A Poki or Yandex adapter is one more file.
  • Ad pacing: a midgame ad only after at least 2 rounds and 120 s of play, at most every 180 s. The rewarded hint is offered only after a lost round, next to an equal "No thanks" button.
  • Low, medium and high quality tiers picked from the device, with an automatic step-down after 4 s under 40 fps.
  • A build check that fails on absolute paths, more than 1,500 files, more than 20 MB, or any outbound URL other than the portal SDK.
  • Cover art rendered from the game at 1920×1080, 800×1200 and 800×800.

How it was measured: the portal build served locally with gzip and opened in headless Chromium with the cache off, three runs per network profile (2.48 to 2.50 s and 6.57 to 6.64 s); frame timings from the renderer on a laptop with a 60 Hz display.

Online netcodethree.jsColyseus on Fly.io

Floor 42: an online duel that stays fair

A side-on 1v1 shootout where the server, or the host's browser, runs the same simulation as the client and decides every hit. The bot and the online mode share one rules engine.

60 Hzfixed-step simulation, same on server and client
30/ssnapshots from the authority
150 mscap on lag-compensation rewind
0.1–0.3units between where you were hit and where you saw yourself on a bad network (was 0.8–1.4)
3bot difficulty levels
Phonestouch controls, landscape and full screen

What was built

  • A deterministic simulation: fixed 1/60 s steps, a seeded random generator stored in the game state, and drawing interpolated between ticks so motion stays smooth at any refresh rate.
  • An online mode with client prediction, numbered snapshots and a buffered timeline for the rival, plus a direct peer connection with relay fallback.
  • A lag fix for players in different countries: on a bad network, the median gap between where a player was hit and where they saw themselves fell from 0.8–1.4 units to 0.1–0.3 on direct connections.
  • Touch controls, landscape layout and full screen on phones.

How it was measured: network rates from the game’s own simulation settings; the hit gap from play over an injected bad network (80 to 180 ms latency, 20 to 60 ms jitter, 1 to 5% loss) on direct connections.

Battle royalethree.jsBots + online rooms

Last Commit Standing: twenty players in a browser tab

A top-down battle royale with a parachute drop, loot, a river to swim and a closing zone. You play solo against nineteen bots; online rooms, where friends join by link and bots take the empty places, are built and coming soon.

0.9 sto a ready main menu on fast 4G
1.4 sfrom Play to the first frame of the round
2.6 sto the menu on slow 4G, emulated phone with a 4× slower CPU
1.6 MBthe whole game over the wire
20players per round, bots fill the empty places
6weapons, from pistol to rockets

What was built

  • A simulation that runs the whole round (drop, loot, zone, bots) separately from the renderer, so the same rules drive solo and online play.
  • Online rooms of up to ten people, filled to twenty with bots, where the host's browser runs the round and a small lobby server on Fly.io connects players.
  • Characters animated with two-bone IK for arms and legs, and a generated countryside with grass, trees and wind.
  • A virtual stick and thumb buttons, with a camera and HUD that fit a phone.

How it was measured: the portal build served with gzip and opened in headless Chromium with the cache off, three runs per network profile (fast 4G: 9 Mbps, 85 ms; slow 4G: 1.6 Mbps, 150 ms).
